I've also been working with the MIT Gas Turbine Lab on novel method for the preliminary design of small jet engines, such as those found on missiles. I was tasked with identifying ways to improve the performance of these small engines, and after some review I focused in on improving compressor design in order to improve thrust to weight ratio. By using a first-principles method which captured the coupling of engine geometry to cycle parameters, I was able to show that a mixed flow compressor offered the best TWR at a small cost to range. This is a significant departure from the radial compressors typically used in modern small jet engines, and my lab is planning to pursue this research direction further in the coming year. As a concurrent side project, I have also undertaken the construction of a 70 newton thrust micro jet engine using this compressor design methodology. I have completed CAD and FEA of the engine and plan to build it over the next few months.

One interesting project that I've completed the design, construction, and successful flight testing of an RC tiltrotor VTOL aircraft. The design involved the creation of a large mathematical model of the aircraft which was used to optimize the aircraft's main sizing parameters, and was then followed by more detailed design iteration with a few aerodynamic analysis tools and Solidworks. I settled on an aircraft with two gimbaling propellers at the wingtips and a single aerodynamic elevator on the tail for control in both hover and forward flight, but due to the complexity of the control algorithms required I had to build and program the main flight electronics from scratch, which included the flight controller and battery pack. After working out some kinks with complex cross-coupling of different control axes in the transition to forward flight, I was able to demonstrate a smooth transition and controlled flight in both vertical and forward flight.
